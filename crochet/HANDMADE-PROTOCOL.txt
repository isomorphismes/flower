Status: PROTOCOL READY. ACTUAL HANDMADE CONSTRUCTION AND MEASUREMENTS NOT_RUN.
No filled-in observation, specimen, photo, physical curvature or phone result
is supplied by this file. A human construction step is necessary.

Use the exact generated INSTRUCTIONS.txt for the increasing ledger, plus the
control instructions, from the same accepted source/artifact revision. The
human summary is in crochet/README.md. Preserve the artifact digest and the
instruction file with the sample. Terms are US: single crochet, chain, slip
stitch. Do not substitute a different stitch convention without recording it.

Recipe cross-check before beginning:
Foundation: eight chains, untwisted ring, slip join into F0.
Round0: one single crochet per foundation chain, total8.
Round1: alternate one and two single crochets into successive parents, total12.
Round2: same parent rule, total18. Round3: same rule, total27; stop.
Each round starts with one non-counting lifting chain and ends with a
non-counting joining slip stitch into its first single crochet. Do not turn.
The control replaces each increasing round with one child per parent:8,8,8,8.
Both children of an increase count. The next round after27 is unspecified.

1. Record the construction conditions.

Record maker identifier and date, instruction revision/digest, yarn product,
fiber composition, nominal yarn size, number of strands, hook size, stitch
convention, working-loop convention, joining method and whether blocked or
washed. Use the same yarn, hook and loop convention for the two samples.
Record any deviation immediately. Do not edit the ledger to conceal a mistaken
increase or counted join; either remake that portion or identify the variant.

2. Recount and identify the material.

Keep the first-stitch marker. Global increasing IDs run S0..S64. Row starts
are S0,S8,S20,S38; row ends S7,S19,S37,S64. Use the emitted incidence diagram
to locate parents and second children. Distinct foundation IDs are F0..F7.
Record each row count and increase count:8/0,12/4,18/6,27/9. The counted total
is65 and total increases19. Control row starts are S0,S8,S16,S24, total32.
Have a second person recount if available; otherwise record that the recount
was by the same maker. Do not label this independent external verification.

3. Record an unloaded state and a declared repeatable measurement method.

Let the fabric rest under the recorded conditions. State whether supported on
a table, loosely held, suspended, or blocked. Record loading as unloaded or
state the known applied force and how it was applied. If using a mass, record
the mass and apparatus instead of silently treating grams as force units.
Make at least three repeat measurements with repositioning. Record resolution,
range and uncertainty; preserve raw values, not only a fitted average.

Measure along material paths using a flexible nonstretch thread or tape. For
each row measure its full circumference along the named single-crochet row,
including the declared seam path. Measure short marked arcs between named IDs
and repeated transverse paths between rows. Define each path on the incidence
diagram so that another person can reproduce it. A parent-to-child path is
not automatically a constant-theta meridian of the synthetic chart.

Keep three quantities separate: path length along the fabric, R3 straight-line
distance between markers, and distance in a photograph. Do not use a flattened
photograph or diameter times pi as a substitute for an intrinsic circumference.

4. Compare calibration choices without turning them into observations.

The current software used synthetic a=pi/16 and h=log(3/2) in arbitrary length
units. Those values were not measured. Fit or choose a length scale only after
recording raw sample dimensions. One comparison may use a=C0/8 and predict
C1=12a,C2=18a,C3=27a; report the three residuals and repeated-measurement ranges.
Check separately whether a roughly constant transverse row spacing is supported.
The exponential interpolation and its curvature remain model assumptions.
Four circumference measurements alone do not establish constant Gaussian
curvature, a unique smooth metric or a calibrated constitutive law.

5. Manipulate and examine the object.

Record whether the same marked material paths keep their lengths, within the
measurement uncertainty and stated loading, while the sample is gently folded,
opened and released. Describe folds, contact, spring-back and persistent creases.
Repeat selected paths after manipulation. A change in 3D shape with unchanged
material lengths is evidence about this specimen's flexibility; it is not proof
of exact isometry. Do not force the object into the supplied revolution shape.

Photograph the same named markers with a scale and a written state label if
useful, including seam and both sides. Photos document appearance and marker
placement; they cannot alone certify intrinsic distances or curvature.
Retain the sample so another person can manipulate and inspect it.

The handmade step passes when the exact construction can be followed and
recounted, raw geometry/loading observations are traceable, deviations are
explicit, and the object is available for physical inspection. Model mismatch
is a meaningful experimental result; it must not be hidden to obtain PASS.
Claiming a constant-curvature physical metric or validated yarn mechanics
requires additional evidence beyond this bounded construction acceptance.
